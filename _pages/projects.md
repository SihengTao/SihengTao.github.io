## Current Work

I use computational approaches, particularly deep learning, to study how cells’ past experiences shape their future behavior and fate, and whether we can reshape these lasting effects to improve cell function. I pursue these questions in Dr. Bo Xia’s lab at the Broad Institute of MIT and Harvard, in collaboration with experimental researchers.

My current work focuses on reversing age-associated, T-cell-intrinsic dysfunction, understanding AP-1’s role in epigenetic memory, and connecting candidate regulators such as ZNF219 to testable mechanisms through genomic data and predictive models.

More broadly, I enjoy exploring diverse biological questions. For example, I also study how WNT8A alternative splicing may contribute to forebrain development in hominoids.

## Previous Work

At Sichuan University, I studied associations between childhood adversity and later-life lung cancer risk with Dr. Xuelei Ma, approaching the lasting effects of past experiences from an epidemiological perspective. I also contributed to research on digital smoking-cessation interventions.

With Dr. Chongde Wu, I used transcriptomic analyses to investigate the antibacterial effects of bacteriocin L14 on _Listeria monocytogenes_.
